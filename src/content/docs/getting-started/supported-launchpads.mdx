Lunchpad drives every Launchpad model listed below, and the Launchkey Mini keyboards. It finds and remembers the right device no matter what the operating system calls its ports.

| Model | Layout in Lunchpad | Colours | Velocity | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Launchpad MK2 | 8 × 8 grid, top row, right column | RGB | No | <Badge text="Tested on hardware" variant="success" /> |
| Launchpad X | 8 × 8 grid, top row, right column | RGB | Yes, plus pressure | <Badge text="Tested on hardware" variant="success" /> |
| Launchpad Mini MK3 | 8 × 8 grid, top row, right column | RGB | No | <Badge text="Not yet tested" variant="caution" /> |
| Launchpad Pro MK2 | 8 × 8 grid with buttons on all four sides | RGB | Yes, plus pressure | <Badge text="Tested on hardware" variant="success" /> |
| Launchpad Pro MK3 | 8 × 8 grid, buttons on all sides, two bottom rows | RGB | Yes, plus pressure | <Badge text="Tested on hardware" variant="success" /> |
| Launchpad S, Launchpad Mini (MK1 and MK2) | 8 × 8 grid, top row, right column | Red and green | No | <Badge text="Tested on hardware" variant="success" /> |
| Launchpad (original, first generation) | 8 × 8 grid, top row, right column | Red and green | No | <Badge text="Not yet tested" variant="caution" /> |

"Not yet tested" means the model is built from Novation's programmer manual but nobody has confirmed it on a real device yet. If you own one, a report on [Discord](https://discord.gg/4Ys9TRR) or [GitHub](https://github.com/ruohki/lunchpad/issues) helps a lot.

The **Launchpad Pro MK3** is confirmed on real hardware (firmware 4.8.3): pads, LEDs and the buttons around the grid. It no longer matters what mode a DAW left the device in — Lunchpad takes it out of that mode first, so a Pro MK3 that used to need switching by hand just connects.

The buttons around the grid work like any other pad: you can put buttons and faders on them. They are drawn the way the hardware looks — a black button whose printed symbol lights up, rather than the whole button — and the corner where the Launchpad has no button (or its logo LED) holds the settings gear in the window.

<Aside title="Telling the third generation apart">
Novation's manuals print the same identification reply for the Launchpad X, Mini MK3 and Pro MK3, but the devices answer with a family of their own. Lunchpad reads the model out of that reply, so a Mini MK3 or Pro MK3 on a port with an unhelpful name is recognised as itself instead of as a Launchpad X. Between those three, a port name that clearly names one of them wins over the reply.
</Aside>

| Launchkey Mini MK4 | 16 pads, Track ∧ and ∨, ▶ and ●, Arp, Scale, Shift, mode arrows, 8 encoders, 2 touch strips, 25 keys, screen | RGB pads, white ▶ and ● | Yes, plus pressure | <Badge text="Tested on hardware" variant="success" /> |

A Launchkey is a keyboard, so it works a little differently: the knobs and touch strips carry faders instead of buttons, and the keys can run macros. See [Launchkey keyboards](/guides/launchkey/).

Both keyboards are confirmed on real hardware (the MK4 with firmware 1.1.9.92): pads, buttons, knobs, touch strips, keys and LEDs.

## When a device is not found

Lunchpad asks every MIDI device to identify itself, and falls back to the name of the port. A device that answers neither — an unusual clone, a port renamed by a driver, a device behind a MIDI interface — can still be driven: pick the model and the two MIDI ports yourself under **Not listed? Connect by hand.** in the device list. See [Connect by hand](/getting-started/first-launch/#not-listed-connect-by-hand).

## Velocity and pressure

On velocity-sensitive models, Lunchpad knows how hard you hit a pad. Actions can use it: a sound can play quieter on a soft press (**Volume follows velocity**), and every action can read the `velocity` placeholder. Models without velocity report every press as full force (127). See [Variables and placeholders](/guides/variables/).

The same models send pressure (aftertouch) while you hold a pad, available as the `pressure` placeholder.

Very soft touches on a velocity-sensitive pad are ignored, so a pad does not fire when you brush it. The default threshold is 25 (out of 127). You can change it in **Settings → Launchpad → Custom press threshold**.

<Aside type="caution" title="Launchpad Pro: velocity comes from the Pro itself">
On a Launchpad Pro, the press force comes from the pad's own set-up screen. Hold **Setup** on the device and pick the velocity option (Low, Med, High or Off) in the third grid row from the top. With **Off**, every press counts as full force.
</Aside>

## Red and green Launchpads

The Launchpad S, the Launchpad Mini MK1 and MK2 and the original Launchpad have red and green LEDs only. On these, Lunchpad:

- offers the 16 colours the device can show instead of the full palette,
- draws the pads on screen the way the LEDs will look (a pure blue pad stays dark),
- shows **Flashing** normally, but shows **Pulsing** as a steady colour, because the hardware cannot pulse.

## Using pages on another model

Pages are not tied to one model. Buttons keep their position when you connect a different Launchpad.

If a page has buttons where the connected Launchpad has no pad (for example the side buttons of a Pro on a Launchpad X), those buttons are kept but not shown. Lunchpad tells you with a notice, and **Settings → Pages & backup** lists how many buttons each page has outside the grid. They come back when you connect a Launchpad with a bigger grid.

You do not have to switch devices to reach them: **Show them** on the notice, or **Buttons out of sight…** in the right-click menu of any pad, opens a list you can drag them from onto free pads. See [the main window](/guides/main-window/#buttons-out-of-sight).

<Aside>
The Launchpad Pro MK2 numbers its pads from the edge of its outer ring of buttons. A page built on an 8 × 8 model therefore lands one pad up and to the right on a Pro MK2.
</Aside>
