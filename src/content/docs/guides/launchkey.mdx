A Launchkey is a keyboard, not a Launchpad, and Lunchpad drives it as one: the pads and the buttons around them take buttons like any grid, the keys can trigger macros, and the knobs and touch strips drive [faders](/guides/faders/).

<AppShot name="launchkey" alt="A Launchkey Mini MK3 in Lunchpad: the two touch strips and the Shift button on the left, eight knobs across the top with a fader on the first, sixteen pads with scene buttons beside them, and the 25 keys along the bottom." />

Connect it like any device: plug it in, pick it in the device list. Lunchpad switches it into DAW mode while it is connected and switches it back when you disconnect or quit.

## What each part does

| Part | What you can do with it |
| --- | --- |
| The 16 pads | Buttons, in full colour. They are velocity sensitive, so `velocity` works. |
| Scene buttons (**>** and **Stop Solo Mute**) | Buttons, in full colour. |
| **Play** and **Record** | Buttons. They have a white LED only, so a colour becomes its brightness. |
| **Shift** | A button, without a light. |
| The 8 knobs and the two touch strips | One [fader](/guides/faders/) each, see below. |
| The 25 keys | Buttons, without a light: pressing the key runs the macro. |
| **Transpose**, **Octave +/−**, **Arp**, **Fixed Chord** | Nothing. The keyboard handles these itself and does not tell Lunchpad about them, so they are drawn greyed out. |

## Faders on knobs and strips

A knob or a strip holds exactly one fader; the hardware supplies the value, so there is nothing to light and no length to pick.

Right-click a knob or a strip and choose **Assign a fader…**. Give it a range and add the actions that should follow the level, exactly as for a fader made of pads. Once it has one, the same menu offers **Edit fader…** and **Remove fader**.

<AppShot name="fader-knob" alt="The fader editor opened for a knob: the title Knob, a Name and Variable name field, the range fields with the Invert button, and a note that the hardware supplies the value." />

- Turning the knob or sliding a finger along the strip moves the fader over its range and runs its actions. While it moves, Lunchpad paces those actions to a few runs per second, so a fast sweep does not start hundreds of macros.
- Every macro can read the level as `fader.` plus the fader's name, the same as any other fader. See [Variables and placeholders](/guides/variables/).
- The pitch strip springs back to the middle when you let go, so a fader on it returns to the middle too. The modulation strip stays where you left it.

<Aside type="tip">
A knob makes a good volume or brightness control: assign a fader with the range 0 to 100 and add [System audio](/actions/media/system-audio/) or [Home Assistant: set a value](/actions/home-assistant/set-a-value/) with **From variable** `value`.
</Aside>

## The keys

Put a button on a key and playing that key runs its macro. The key still sends its note to anything else listening.

Lunchpad knows the keys at the keyboard's default octave. Press **Octave +** or **Octave −** on the device and the keys send other notes, which Lunchpad ignores, so buttons on keys stop responding until you return to the default octave. With the arpeggiator on, a held key repeats and so does the macro.

<Aside>
Keys have no lights, so the colour of a button on a key only shows in the window.
</Aside>

## Pages across devices

A page made for a Launchpad keeps its buttons when you connect a Launchkey, but the grids are shaped differently, so buttons can land where the Launchkey has no pad. They are kept, and [Out of sight](/guides/main-window/#buttons-out-of-sight) brings them back onto free pads. The same happens the other way round. See [using pages on another model](/getting-started/supported-launchpads/#using-pages-on-another-model).

Buttons that land on a knob or a strip are treated the same way: those cells take a fader, not a button.
