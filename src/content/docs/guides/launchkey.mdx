A Launchkey is a keyboard, not a Launchpad, and Lunchpad drives it as one: the pads and the buttons around them take buttons like any grid, the keys can trigger macros, and the knobs and touch strips drive [faders](/guides/faders/).

Two models are supported: the **Launchkey Mini MK3** and the **Launchkey Mini MK4**.

<AppShot name="launchkey" alt="A Launchkey Mini MK3 in Lunchpad: the two touch strips and Shift on the left, eight knobs across the top, sixteen pads with the two scene buttons beside them, the transport buttons, and the 25 keys along the bottom." />

Connect it like any device: plug it in, pick it in the device list. Lunchpad puts it into DAW mode itself while it is connected, and hands it back when you disconnect or quit. There is nothing to set on the device first.

## Launchkey Mini MK4

The MK4 is drawn the way the front panel is laid out, down to the screen, which Lunchpad uses as the settings button.

<AppShot name="launchkey-mk4" alt="A Launchkey Mini MK4 in Lunchpad: the pitch and modulation strips at the left, the screen showing the settings gear, Shift and the transport buttons, Arp and Scale, eight encoders over the two rows of pads, the track buttons beside them, and the keys along the bottom." />

| Part | What you can do with it |
| --- | --- |
| The 16 pads | Buttons, in full colour. Velocity sensitive **and** pressure sensitive, so both `velocity` and `pressure` work. |
| Track **∧** and **∨** | Buttons, in full colour. |
| **▶** and **●** | Buttons. They have a white LED only, so a colour becomes its brightness. |
| **Shift**, the mode arrows right of the encoders | Buttons, without a light. |
| **Arp** and **Scale** | Buttons, without a light — but tap only, see below. |
| The 8 encoders and the two touch strips | One [fader](/guides/faders/) each, see below. |
| The 25 keys | Buttons, without a light: pressing the key runs the macro. |
| The screen | Lunchpad's settings button. While connected, the screen shows the Lunchpad logo. |
| **Settings**, **Oct −**, **Oct +**, **>**, **Func** | Nothing. The keyboard keeps these for itself, so they are drawn greyed out. |

### Arp and Scale report a tap only

The device reports these two as a toggle, once, and never reports a release. Lunchpad switches the function straight back off and adds the release itself, so a button on **Arp** or **Scale** has a pressed list only — no released actions, no held actions, and no hold time. The button editor says so:

> This button reports a tap only: the device sends no release, so there are no released or held actions.

### The surface stays Lunchpad's

While the MK4 is connected, Lunchpad keeps the pads in the layout it drives and the encoders in the mode it reads. If you change the pad layout or the encoder mode at the device, or switch **Arp** or **Scale** on there, Lunchpad puts it back. Disconnect to use the keyboard as a keyboard again.

| Part | What you can do with it |

| The 16 pads | Buttons, in full colour. They are velocity sensitive, so `velocity` works. |

| **▶** and **●** | Buttons. They have a white LED only, so a colour becomes its brightness. |

| The 25 keys | Buttons, without a light: pressing the key runs the macro. |

<Aside>
A button the keyboard keeps for itself is drawn dashed and dimmed, and hovering it says *"(keyboard function, not usable here)"*. You cannot put a button on it.
</Aside>

## Faders on knobs and strips

A knob, an encoder or a touch strip holds exactly one fader; the hardware supplies the value, so there is nothing to light and no length to pick.

Right-click one and choose **Assign a fader…** (a plain click on a free knob does the same). Give it a range and the actions that should follow the level, exactly as for a fader made of pads. Once it has one, the same menu offers **Edit fader…** and **Remove fader**.

<AppShot name="fader-knob" alt="The fader editor opened for a knob: the title Knob, a Name and Variable name field, the range fields with the Invert button, and a note that the hardware supplies the value." />

- Turning the knob or sliding a finger along the strip moves the fader over its range. The level follows your hand at once; the actions run once it comes to rest, so a fast sweep does not start hundreds of macros.
- Every macro can read the level as `fader.` plus the fader's name, like any other fader. See [Variables and placeholders](/guides/variables/).
- The pitch strip springs back to the middle, so Lunchpad draws its level as a bar rather than a fill. The modulation strip stays where you left it and is drawn as a fill.

### A touch strip has three lists

A strip is not just a slider you can read: it knows when a finger lands on it and when it leaves. Its **Actions** tab has three lists instead of one.

<AppShot name="fader-strip-actions" alt="The Actions tab of a fader on a touch strip: the three lists When touched, While moving and When let go, with an OBS audio action in each." />

| List | Runs… |
| --- | --- |
| **When touched** | when a finger lands on the strip, with the first value. |
| **While moving** | on every value while the finger stays down. |
| **When let go** | when the finger leaves. |

All three get the level as `value`, `percent`, `fraction`, `step`, `steps` and `display`, and `{{trigger}}` tells a shared macro which one is running (`faderTouch`, `fader` or `faderRelease`).

The two strips report the release differently:

- The **modulation** strip counts as let go once it has rested for a moment. The released actions get the last value you touched, and the level stays there.
- The **pitch** strip reports the release itself, and the actions get **the value you let go at**, not the middle. The spring back to the middle moves the level but runs nothing.

<Aside type="tip">
This is what makes a strip a push-to-talk: unmute **When touched**, follow the level **While moving**, mute again **When let go**.
</Aside>

## The keys

Put a button on a key and playing that key runs its macro. The key still sends its note to anything else listening.

Lunchpad knows the keys at the keyboard's default octave (C2 to C4). Press **Octave +** or **Octave −** on the device and the keys send other notes, which Lunchpad ignores, so buttons on keys stop responding until you return to the default octave. Lunchpad cannot see the octave change, because those buttons are the keyboard's own.

On the MK3, with the device's arpeggiator on, a held key repeats — and so does the macro.

<Aside>
Keys have no lights, so the colour of a button on a key only shows in the window.
</Aside>

## When the keys and strips stay silent

The pads, buttons and encoders arrive on the keyboard's DAW port, which is the one the device list shows. The **keys and the two touch strips arrive on its ordinary MIDI port**, which Lunchpad opens alongside it. If that port is missing or another program holds it, everything else keeps working while the keys and strips report nothing.

On Windows only one program can hold a MIDI port at a time: close your DAW, Novation Components or another controller app, then connect again. See [Troubleshooting](/help/troubleshooting/#a-launchkeys-keys-or-touch-strips-do-nothing).

## Pages across devices

A page made for a Launchpad keeps its buttons when you connect a Launchkey, but the grids are shaped differently, so buttons can land where the Launchkey has no pad. They are kept, and [Out of sight](/guides/main-window/#buttons-out-of-sight) brings them back onto free pads. The same happens the other way round. See [using pages on another model](/getting-started/supported-launchpads/#using-pages-on-another-model).

Buttons that land on a knob or a strip are treated the same way: those cells take a fader, not a button.

<Aside type="caution" title="How far these are tested">
The **MK4's presses** are confirmed on real hardware (firmware 1.1.9.92); its LEDs are built from Novation's manual and not confirmed yet. The **MK3** is built from the manual and community notes and has not been on a device at all. If you own either, a report on [Discord](https://discord.gg/4Ys9TRR) or [GitHub](https://github.com/ruohki/lunchpad/issues) helps a lot.
</Aside>
